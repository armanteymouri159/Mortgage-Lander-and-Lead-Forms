How a cash-out refi actually works

When you refinance your mortgage and take cash out, you're borrowing against the equity you've built in your home. Your existing mortgage gets replaced with a new one — at a new rate — and the difference comes to you as cash.

"You're not getting a second loan on top of your mortgage — you're replacing it with a new one that frees up equity you've already built."

That cash can go directly toward wiping out high-interest debt. Many homeowners use it to clear credit cards, personal loans, medical bills, or any mix of expensive monthly obligations. And because mortgage rates are typically much lower than consumer debt rates, the monthly savings can be significant.

Who this works best for

A cash-out refinance isn't the right move for everyone — but for homeowners who fit the profile, it can be a powerful reset. It tends to work well when:

  • ✓ You're carrying high-interest credit card or personal loan debt
  • ✓ You have meaningful equity built up in your home
  • ✓ The new monthly payment would be lower than what you're paying now across all debts combined
  • ✓ You want a predictable, fixed-rate payment
  • ✓ You plan to stay in the home long enough to benefit

Credit score, home equity, income, property type, location, and program availability can all affect your rate and eligibility.

What about credit score?

The minimum bar is lower than many expect.

While perfect credit isn't required, lenders typically look for a 580 or higher. The better your credit, the better your rate — but many programs are available even for borrowers who've had some credit challenges. What matters most is the equity position and your overall financial picture.

Common questions

Does this replace my existing mortgage?
Yes. A cash-out refinance pays off your current mortgage and replaces it with a new loan at today's rate. You also receive the cash-out amount at closing.
How much cash can I access?
Generally up to 80% of your home's value, minus what you still owe. The exact amount depends on your equity, credit, and the lender's program guidelines.
What credit score do I need?
Most programs start at 580, with better rates available above 620. The higher your score, the more competitive your options tend to be.
Can I use the cash for anything?
Yes — most lenders don't restrict how you use cash-out proceeds. Common uses include paying off high-interest debt, home improvements, or general financial flexibility.
How long does the process take?
Timelines vary by lender and loan complexity, but many cash-out refis close in 30 to 45 days. Checking your options takes about 60 seconds.
Will checking my options affect my credit?
Checking your options through this process won't affect your credit score. A hard inquiry typically only occurs when you formally apply with a lender.
